## 1. What Ubuntu is

Ubuntu is a clothing brand founded in Kigali by a designer who hand-makes every
piece. The name is the Nguni Bantu philosophy — *"Umuntu ngumuntu ngabantu"*: a
person is a person through other persons. I am because we are. The community of
customers is called **Abantu** — "the people," the literal meaning of the word
made real. (Abantu carries this meaning in Kinyarwanda exactly as it does in Zulu;
Kinyarwanda is itself a Bantu language.)

**A precision the brand had not noticed, found in Round 14.** *Umuntu ngumuntu
ngabantu* is a **Nguni** maxim — Zulu and Xhosa — not a Rwandan one. The word
*ubuntu* itself, however, is fully Kinyarwanda, meaning humanity and human
generosity, and *gira ubuntu* — "have consideration, be humane" — is living
everyday speech in Rwanda. So the name is native and the maxim is borrowed. The
copy rule requires references to be named precisely, so the story page names it as
Nguni rather than leaving it silent. Whether a Rwandan expression is carried
alongside it is **his call** (Section 13).

**Brand promise:** *"Ubuntu dresses the African who knows who they are — and wears
that knowledge beautifully."*

The ambition is an ecosystem, not a shop — a world people belong to, where the
clothes are one way in. Themes change with every collection; the DNA never does.

It is a **partnership** (Section 4) and the model is **made-to-order** (Section 5).

## 2. The product & the signature

- **The signature:** thin, single-line hand embroidery — continuous-line drawings
  stitched onto garments. Not patterns, but *scenes*: figures fishing, boats on
  water, people at a shoreline, everyday human life. Every piece carries a small
  illustrated story.
- **Current pieces:** bucket hats, embroidered jacket with contrast collar,
  two-tone vest with landscape-cut colour blocking, shorts with fishing/boat
  scenes. **Four designs at launch** — presented honestly, not padded.
- **First theme:** *Summer — life by the water.*
- **Why it is hard to copy:** uniqueness lives in two places at once — *craft*
  (slow, hand-stitched, every piece slightly different) and *narrative* (each
  garment holds a named scene, each release explained).

## 5. The business model

**Made-to-order, year one.** No stock, no cart, no instant checkout. Every piece
is made after it is ordered — *someone made this for me* is literally true.

**Two tiers:**
1. **The Collection** — his designs, orderable by anyone; each order hand-stitched
   fresh. The engine: repeatable, steady, scalable.
2. **Commissions ("Only yours")** — a customer brings their own scene; he creates a
   design for them alone, priced meaningfully higher. The crown: rare, personal,
   always his hands.

**Order flow:** Request → Confirm → Pay → Make.

### Pricing — resolved

It is a **price, not a quote.** The number on the page is what they pay.

- Every piece carries a **base price**, shown on the card and the page.
- Options — cut, size, colourway — may carry a **modifier** (default zero, can be
  negative). Priority is a modifier.
- **The base price must be the ordinary configuration**, not the cheapest edge
  case. Otherwise the card price is fiction.
- Everything that costs more is shown **while it is being chosen**, so the button
  always shows the real total.
- His confirmation message then confirms what they saw. A different number becomes
  a genuine exception, not routine — which is what makes the no-surprises promise
  real rather than stated.

**Commissions — three gates** *(Round 6; supersedes the earlier deposit-percentage
model).* A commission has no total until the design is agreed, so a percentage
upfront is arithmetically impossible. Instead:

> **Design fee** (absolute figure, paid before he drafts) → **payment to start
> cutting** (once the design is agreed) → **balance on completion**, before it ships.

The design fee is **credited against the total**, and that treatment is uniform even
where the amount varies. The order snapshots an ordered payment schedule; a
collection order simply has one entry. Which gives the model in one line:

> **A collection order is a commission with one gate.**

**Delivery:** free within Rwanda, absorbed into base prices, no line item.
International later, as a **destination-zone modifier shown while it is being
chosen** — not at the confirmation step. *(Corrected in the pre-build review: R3's
rule is that the button always shows the real total, and R15 chose the modifier
specifically so shipping from Kigali could neither inflate the base price for Rwandan
buyers nor appear as a surprise at confirmation.)*

**Priority — sold as position, never speed.** A normal order takes its honest
place in the queue; a priority order pays more to move nearer the front. Every
piece is stitched at full, unrushed care. This is enforced structurally: priority
changes the queue offset, never the making days (Section 11H).

**Fit system** *(revised in Round 7).* **Measurements are the urged path** — the
site asks for them and teaches how to take them. Standard sizing (S/M/L/XL plus
height and weight as sanity checks) remains fully available and is never removed.
Where a piece misfits because the numbers given were wrong, alteration is offered
at a stated additional price. When a physical measuring point exists, the liability
model already accounts for it.

**Cut, not gender.** What changes the making is the cut of the garment, not who
the customer is. Cut is a per-piece option, structurally identical to colourway —
he sets the available cuts on each piece; a piece with one cut asks no question.
There is no gender field anywhere on the site.

## 6. The scaling model — his hands, his standard

The entire premise rests on one sentence being literally true: *someone made this
for me, by hand, with care.* Growth must not quietly break it.

- **His hands stay on the soul — forever.** The stitched scene is his, or overseen
  by him.
- **His standard governs the rest.** Cutting, fabric prep, base construction —
  skilled labour trained hands can do under his standard.
- **The honest promise:** *"Designed by him, its scene stitched or overseen by him,
  made to his standard by Ubuntu's hands."*
- **Structure:** the Collection is the atelier; Commissions stay his alone, forever.
- **The rule that keeps it Ubuntu — transparency.** Makers are **named and
  celebrated** — faces on the story page, "stitched by ___ in our Kigali workshop."
  Named openly, it lives the Collective Greatness value.

**Foresight for the site now, and only this:** the maker is a **table with a foreign
key from the order** — one row on day one, his, and ready for a second name the day it
is true. *(Round 16 replaced a typed string with the table: this page promises makers
will be named and celebrated, and the day that page exists a typed name per order is a
backfill of misspellings. The dashboard price is a makers list in Settings and a picker
on the order, costed into Phase 2.)*

**The timing gap.** Hiring runs on months; demand spikes run on days. And the part
he cannot delegate is the slow part. So the site absorbs the lag through timeframes
(Section 11H), not through turning people away.

## 7. Payments — rewritten July 2026

### The hard constraint

**Every payment gateway in Rwanda requires business registration.** Rwandan PSPs
require proof of registration, tax compliance certificates and identification
during onboarding, with KYC verification before activation. IremboPay does not
offer self-onboarding at all.

Until RDB registration happens, **manual mobile money is the only legal option.**
The launch flow is not a compromise — it is the only thing available.

### Now

MTN MoMo / Airtel Money, manual. The order's private page shows payment details
with the order reference; the customer can report payment with a transaction
reference; he confirms it in the dashboard.

### eKash — what it actually is

**eKash is a rail, not a gateway you integrate with.** Operated by RSwitch, built
on Mojaloop, connecting 22 institutions including MTN and Airtel. Full transition
completed **14 July 2026** under BNR Directive No. 45/2026 — every domestic transfer
between banks and wallets now runs through it.

- Rail fee capped at **RWF 20 regardless of value**; up to RWF 10M per transaction.
- Person-to-merchant is live: customers can pay a merchant from any bank or wallet
  regardless of which institution issued the merchant's code.
- **You reach it through an acquirer.** Bank of Kigali and MVend Limited were the
  first merchant acquirers.
- The RWF 20 is the fee **on the rail** — not what an acquirer charges a merchant.
  Those are different layers.
- QR-code merchant payments appear in planned expansions; earlier claims that "one
  QR code accepts everything" may be ahead of reality. Verify directly.

**Why this matters more than it looks.** For high-value, low-volume made-to-order,
percentage gateways are brutal: a 3% fee takes 6,000 RWF from a 200,000 commission.
The rail costs 20. So the gateway question is really **"which acquirer has the
thinnest merchant markup on eKash"** — a phone call to BK and MVend, not a research
task. Worth making early; it may change which bank he uses.

### Alternatives if that fails

- **Flutterwave** — licensed in Rwanda, fast onboarding, mobile money and cards,
  popular with startups.
- **IremboPay** — BNR-licensed and PCI-DSS compliant, RWF/USD/EUR/GBP, but no
  self-onboarding, T-2 settlements Wednesdays and Fridays, negotiated pricing,
  setup fees possible.
- **K-Pay** — home-grown Rwandan gateway, local support, fast onboarding.

**Paystack does not serve Rwanda.** It is part of Stripe and covers Nigeria, Ghana,
South Africa and Kenya. Stripe itself is not available to Rwanda-based merchants
without a foreign company. Do not plan around either.

### On the site

Payment is recorded as **how** as well as whether — manual or gateway. Manual is a
**permanent capability**, not a permanent presentation: once a gateway is live it
becomes the visible path, and manual is the fallback for outages and customers who
insist. Removing the capability means an outage stops orders.

## 8. Marketing & market entry

- **Starting point:** near-zero audience. Every first sale is won from strangers.
- **First buyers:** Rwanda first; international later.
- **The strategy:** the story *is* the marketing. Because payment is upfront and
  nothing sits on a shelf, strangers must trust the craft before the piece exists
  — trust built by showing process: hands stitching, scenes taking shape.
- **Format:** short-form video (Reels / TikTok). Over 70% of social engagement in
  Rwanda is short-form video, and these platforms show new accounts to strangers
  based on the video, not follower count.
- **Accounts:** free Business accounts on TikTok and Instagram. Instagram carries
  the site link from day one; TikTok link-in-bio needs 1,000 followers or business
  verification.
- **Editing:** CapCut free tier. **Ad spend:** none until after the first sale.
- **Handle:** "ubuntu" is a common word and famous software — secure a distinct
  handle across platforms. This is decided together with the domain.
- **Faces:** he is the face and hands on camera.
- **Mobile-first** because most traffic arrives on phones from TikTok and Instagram.

## 9. Visual identity

**The line is the language.** The single-line embroidery is not just shown in
photos — it is how the site is drawn and how it moves. Scenes redraw as line art;
dividers are running stitches; hovers are stitches sewing themselves. The site
should feel drawn by the same hand that stitches the garments.

**Typography:** the handwritten script stays sacred — logo only, plus one closing
mark per page. Around it, a warm literary serif for headings and a clean humanist
sans-serif for body and forms. No decorative "African-themed" display fonts.

**The logo.** Handwritten lowercase *"ubuntu."* — one flowing connected line, a
long "t" crossbar sweeping through the word, ending in a full stop. Presented on
a wine-dark ground.

- Its anatomy is already a choreography: the word writes itself → the crossbar
  sweeps through → the dot lands.
- **Option (his call):** on the site only, the final dot lands in Wine — the one
  red in the sequence, like a knot tied at the end of a thread.
- **Needs vectorising:** a lettering artist should trace the script into clean SVG
  paths in writing order. Until then, trace well enough to prototype.
- **Open:** is the wine-dark background an intentional brand colour or just a
  background he used?

## 10. Motion charter

**The four laws** — every animation obeys these:

1. **Vertical, never sideways.** Elements enter by rising.
2. **Stillness between movements.** Nothing idles or loops. A thing animates once,
   deliberately, then rests. The running-stitch divider is the one exception.
3. **The cloth follows the line.** Colour or image settles a fraction after the
   line or heading carrying it — reads as fabric, as weight.
4. **One thread at a time.** Never three things animating at once.

Every motion decision is locked as *intent* and tuned against real code during
development. All motion honours reduced-motion settings and uses native browser
APIs and SVG stroke animation — no libraries. That constraint is both aesthetic
and technical (Section: `ubuntu-technical.md`).

**The eight moments** — full detail in `.claude/rules/motion.md`.

1. **First arrival — LOCKED.** Cream screen. The signature writes itself (~2s),
   then rises to the header as the page lifts. Once per session; any interaction
   skips; reduced motion sees the finished signature.
2. **Page transitions — LOCKED.** One persistent wine nav stitch that sews itself
   between nav items. Outgoing exhales down while fading; incoming rises from
   below, staggered. ~500ms. One richer transition for gallery → product: the
   image lifts and expands from its card.
3. **Scroll reveals — LOCKED.** Each section rises once (~15px + fade, ~500ms),
   staggered within. Fires once only. Section-level, not paragraph-level. Photos
   uncovered by a rising edge, bottom to top, like cloth lifted off a finished piece.
4. **The scene drawing — sketched.** Scroll-linked line art on product and story
   pages. Commissions plays it in reverse — an empty ground line waiting.
5. **Hovers — sketched.** Underlines that sew themselves; dashed stitch borders
   that run. Must have a touch equivalent.
6. **Dividers — sketched.** Running stitches; the only thing that moves on its own.
7. **Form moments — sketched.** The live fit diagram; gentle field transitions.
8. **The ceremony — sketched.** When an order turns to "In the making," a line
   figure performs a single vertical jump — the adumu. Once, there, nowhere else.

## 11. Content map

All page copy is drafted in his voice and **fully editable in the dashboard**. We
launch with strong drafts in every field. Full text lives in `ubuntu-copy.md`.

### A. Home

1. **Signature hero** — the arrival animation, one customer-facing line, the water
   scene beneath, one button: *See the collection.*
2. **Who we are** — three or four lines, then out of the way. Split layout: text
   left, animation right — one unbroken line draws a figure, then continues into a
   second, third, fourth. **One line becoming a community.** Stacks on mobile.
3. **The collection** — the window display (see B).
4. **The ritual** (see C).
5. **The making — condensed** (see D).
6. **Collective greatness** — the closing section: the Abantu invitation and a
   commissions teaser, one door into the story page.
7. **Footer.**

Order logic: show the beauty → state who we are → earn the trust → offer the meaning.

### B. The collection — a window display, not a catalogue

**This is the resolution of an old contradiction.** "Worlds, not inventory"
implied collections end; made-to-order means nothing ever runs out. So the
homepage collection is not a set that closes — it is a **curated selection of what
he wants people to meet first**, changeable at any time from the dashboard.

- **Theme as headline:** *"Summer — life by the water,"* with *"The current
  collection"* above it.
- **Cards carry four things only:** photo on cream, name (Kinyarwanda), the scene
  in a few words, base price. **The whole card is the door.** No cart.
- One bridge line beneath: *"Every piece is stitched after you ask for it."*
- Desktop 2×2. Mobile stacks full-width.
- **Hover is decorative only, never information** — most visitors are on phones
  and have no hover. A second photo, or a stitch border drawing itself.

**The homepage is a landing page, not the shop** *(Round 10).* The window display
sits inside it, but the page's job is to explain what this is before it asks for
anything. Most arrivals come from a fifteen-second video on a phone knowing nothing
about the brand — they have to learn that nothing is stocked, or a made-to-order
price makes no sense and they leave. A grid homepage is faster for someone who
already knows Ubuntu, which at launch is nobody.

**The full range lives on its own page**, browsable, holding every piece currently
live — and it **sorts newest-first**, which makes it the permanent answer to *what's
new.* One stable URL he can post every time he finishes something, never stale and
never empty. There is no separate "new" page: with four pieces it would be this page
with a different heading, and it would have to be maintained. `published_at` exists
and sorts from day one; the visible *newly stitched* marker waits until the
catalogue earns it, because a badge on all four pieces says nothing. **Never
"drop"** — a drop implies the thing goes away, and nothing here does.

Filter taxonomy — garment type, adult or kids — exists in the schema from day one;
the filter interface is built when the catalogue earns it, roughly 15–20 pieces. A
filter bar above four items makes a small brand look like an empty shop.

**Navigation is four items** — Collection, **Only yours**, The making, Our story —
with policies and Talk to us in the footer and no hamburger. Four fit across a phone,
and a menu that has to be opened costs the click that gets someone into the story.
Revisited in Phase 5 against real Kinyarwanda labels, which run about 30% longer.

**The label and the route differ here, and only here.** The route is `/commissions`,
because *only-yours* is a poor slug and a worse link to paste into a message; the
label a customer reads is **Only yours.** Written down rather than left as an
unwritten rule, which is what it had been.

**Founder homework:** consistent photography (same cream backdrop, same warm light);
Kinyarwanda names for each design, named after its scene, translation on the page.

### C. The ritual section

Full-width on Savanna Tan, **no photo**, one scene as line art drawing itself.
*"Every piece carries a story."* plus ~5 lines, and one door to a real product page.

**Biggest content homework:** a written story per piece (~150 words) — the scene,
its meaning, its reference, in his voice.

### D. The making — its own page

Promoted from section to page; people return to it at different moments.

The homepage keeps a condensed version. The full page holds: the four steps
expanded with the honest *why* at each; the **progress-photo promise** as the trust
masterstroke; the fit system explained; the fit policy stated fairly; and
**timeline honesty** — hand-stitching means weeks, said proudly. Richest process
photography on the site, on cream.

### E. The product page — the key template

**What a piece is:** one garment + one scene, sold as a single design. Colourway,
cut and size are options on it, not separate products. The scene and its story are
fields on the piece — the same scene on two garments is two pieces.

- **Arrival:** card expands into the page. Photograph leads, on cream. Beside it:
  name (Kinyarwanda + translation), the scene in one line, and the **base price
  immediately.** Below: other views plus at least one **extreme close-up of the
  stitching** — the proof.
- **Story (scroll):** the scene redrawn as scroll-linked line art, his ~150 words,
  and a short *making* block. **Story sits above the order block deliberately.**
- **The order block — "Ask for this piece"** (never "Buy now"): colourway, cut,
  the fit fork, a free notes line, contact details. The displayed total updates as
  options are chosen.
  - **Fit fork, path 1:** standard size (S–XL) plus height and weight. A quiet
    *"what do these sizes mean?"* opens the size chart — chest, length, shoulder
    ranges per size.
  - **Fit fork, path 2 ("I have my measurements"):** an annotated line-drawing in
    the site's ink style, measurements drawn onto the garment where the tape goes.
    **The numbers typed appear live on the drawing.** Each field carries guardrails so
    a typo like "9" for "90" is caught before cutting. **The drawing is referenced by
    key from the garment type, not owned by it** *(R13c)* — so *kids 2–6* and *kids
    7–12* point at one file rather than two identical ones that drift the moment
    either is edited.
- **Measurements are urged, and therefore taught.** *(Round 7 corrects an earlier
  assumption here — that anyone with measurements got them from a tailor, so the
  teaching gap was empty. Urging measurements recruits people who have neither
  numbers nor a method, so instruction is now load-bearing.)*
  - **How each measurement is taken ships beside its field**, at the moment of
    entry — not in a tooltip. That sentence is what the shared-liability clause in
    the policy turns on.
  - **Still no separate fit guide page.** Teaching at the point of need beats a page
    nobody opens, and the diagram *is* the guide. One addition: a short, shareable
    **"what your tailor needs"** list, because path 2 routes through a third person
    who needs the list and none of the on-page instructions.
- **A novice's bad measurement is worse than a standard size.** Guardrails catch
  impossible values, not plausible wrong ones — a tape pulled tight, taken over a
  jacket, taken off a garment instead of a body. This is why the founder homework
  below stopped being homework and became a blocker.
- **Beneath the button:** three lines of what happens next.
- **Bottom doors:** fit policy in one fair sentence; link to The making; *"or bring
  us a scene of your own."*

**Founder homework per garment — now blocking, not deferred.** Which measurements;
**how each is taken** (on the body, or garment laid flat); a **plausible** band, an
**impossible** band; and the body ranges for S/M/L/XL. Hand it to him as a table to
fill, not as a conversation.

### F. "Only yours" — commissions

Named "Only yours" in the nav, with scene language inside.

**Its purpose:** sells the second tier (best margin — buying his design mind);
teaches what commissioning means; filters to serious requests via a stated price;
elevates the brand into an atelier with an accessible entrance.

- **Opening:** *"Every Ubuntu piece carries a scene. This one carries yours — and
  no one else's."* **Dark page.** One drawing: the reversed animation — ground line
  drawn, needle waiting, scene absent. The emptiness is the product.
- **Three movements:** *What this is* (designed once, stitched once, retired).
  *How it goes* (a slower, personal conversation, now with three named payment
  moments). *What it costs* — the **design fee**, on the page. A real number for the
  first step is a sharper filter than a starting-from guess for a total nobody can
  yet quote, and it lets the page say what it costs to *begin*.
- **The form asks for a story, not a size.** No measurements here; fit comes later
  in the conversation.
- **Button: "Send it to him."**
- **Payment — three gates:** design fee before he drafts → payment to start cutting
  once the design is agreed → balance on completion. The design fee is credited
  against the total.
- **Ownership transfers on full payment.** Until then the design is his, to retire
  or reuse at his discretion — stated plainly on the page rather than discovered.
  *Only yours* attaches to the finished piece, not to the drawing. A reused design
  is **cloned into a new piece with a new name and a new story**; the customer's
  story is never reused, because it was told in confidence.
- **Soft exit:** *"Not sure yet? Start with the collection."*

### G. The story

A descent from the widest circle to the smallest: **philosophy → brand → maker →
community.** The word and what it means; why a clothing brand (his origin story);
the maker — his name, face, hands, the one page where photography is about him;
Abantu, honest about being small at launch.

**Rule:** readable in three minutes. Depth through precision, not length.

### H. The order journey

**Every order gets its own private page**, carrying a status thread. His reply and
price appear there; the progress photos appear there. The customer is not tracking
a parcel — they are watching a page fill with their piece coming into existence.

**An order is an append-only event log**, not a status field. Each event has a
timestamp and an actor. Status is derived for the dashboard pipeline; the log is
the truth. This gives the customer page, an audit trail, and a guarantee nothing
is silently overwritten.

**Payment is a separate dimension from journey.** Paid-ness is derived from the
payments table — sum received against total. So no payment states clutter the
machine, and "awaiting balance" on a commission needs no new state: the order is
finished, and the page reads *your piece is ready, the balance is due before it ships.*

**The path:**

> Requested → Confirmed → *(In design — commissions only)* → Paid →
> In the making → On its way → Delivered

- **Paid and In the making stay separate.** Paid means *in the queue*; In the
  making means *he has started.* This is the honest version of priority-as-position.
- **Delivered matters:** the alteration window runs from receipt, so without it
  the clock has no start. It carries the welcome into Abantu.
- **Plus three off-path states:** **Declined**, **Lapsed**, **Cancelled.** Lapsed
  is operationally essential — without it, every stranger who requests and vanishes
  sits in Confirmed forever and the pipeline becomes unreadable within months.
- **Customer-reported payment** is a payment record marked *reported*, not an order
  state. It closes the silence between sending money and him noticing.
- **In design** *(Round 6, commissions only)* is derived from the design fee landing.
  The design fee is a payment that does **not** advance the journey — payment as a
  separate dimension doing real work. Without this word a commission would read
  *Confirmed* for three weeks while he is actively drawing, which looks stalled.
- **`making_complete` is an event, not a state.** It earns the finished-but-unshipped
  moment a commission needs, and for every order it separates *he finished stitching*
  from *he got to the post office* — the exact duration wanted when calculated
  timeframes become possible.

**Decline exists permanently, and it is consent, not capacity.** He must be able to
refuse a scene he does not want to stitch. Rarely used; worded as a maker's choice,
not a rejection.

**No self-service cancellation.** A conversation instead. The refund **shrinks as
the work progresses** — nearly all before cutting, less once cloth is cut for their
body, least mid-stitching. The policy states the principle; the number is his per
case. This quietly rewards telling him early.

**Timeframes are typed, not calculated:**
- **Making days, per piece** — a property of the craft, set once.
- **Queue offset, global** — one number he raises when busy; all pieces update at once.
- **Priority offset, global.**
- The customer sees making days + offset.
- **Priority changes the offset, never the making days.** The data model itself
  cannot express "we stitch it faster."
- ~~The dashboard **nudges** when open orders cross a threshold.~~ **Struck in the
  pre-build review** on Round 9a's own reasoning: it would need a number guessed before
  a single real order exists, and Today is never truncated, so capacity is already in
  front of him.
- **The priority option renders only while the queue offset is non-zero.** The modifier
  and the offset exist from day one; the page does not offer a paid place nearer the
  front of a line that is empty.
- The event log records real durations. After 20–30 orders, calculated timeframes
  become possible from history rather than a guess. Nothing extra to build now.

**The order ceremony** (the adumu jump) lives at the moment an order turns to
In the making.

### I. The practical layer — complete

**No fit guide page** (see E). What remains is **policies, contact, footer.**

**Policies** — the full text is in `ubuntu-copy.md`. The structure:

- **Fit.** Three causes of misfit, three different owners. *Their numbers were
  wrong* — theirs. *We cut outside our own stated measurements* — ours, remade or
  repaired free. *Our instructions were unclear and they measured in good faith
  another way* — shared. A single rule covering all fit issues would silently cover
  his mistakes too, and that is the kind of thing a customer discovers once and
  tells everyone about.
- **Alteration — the middle path.** It is not refund-or-nothing. He has the
  pattern and the hands; taking something in is an afternoon. *Send it back, he
  adjusts it, you cover the work and the return — or, outside Rwanda, a stated
  contribution toward a tailor near you instead, because posting it back and forth
  would cost more than the piece.* **Both cases in one sentence, never a
  country-conditional variant** *(pre-build review)*: a variant is a request-time
  branch on an edge-cached page, and the cache key is not worth buying for one clause.
- **Duties are the buyer's, stated plainly** *(R15, which had reached no document).*
  Unstated, it arrives as a courier's bill and reads as a brand that misled them.
- **Your details, and asking us to forget them.** Order pages hold a phone number, an
  address and body measurements, and the law that governs them is Rwanda's Law
  058/2021. A customer can ask to be forgotten: the person is anonymised and **the
  measurements go with them**, while the order itself survives so that past orders
  still resolve. Where a piece was measured for a child, those numbers are the
  parent's request to erase and they clear with it — the child was never a record here
  in the first place.
- **A craft decision this depends on:** **leave generous seam allowance on every
  piece.** Too big is always fixable; too small usually is not. Without it, the
  alteration promise is half a promise.
- **No returns.** A piece cut for one body has no second buyer. Stated, then turned
  into the reason the brand exists.
- **Cancellation before making**, per Section 11H.
- **Hands, not machines.** Small variations are the work of a hand, not a fault.

**Contact — `/contact`, mirrored at `/rw/contact`, built in Phase 3, reached from
the footer and never from the nav.** *(The page was specified here and drafted in full
in `ubuntu-copy.md` §6, and had a URL in neither until the pre-build review.)*

**Its job is to route, not to be reachable.** A wide-open contact page
quietly competes with the order form: someone who could have ordered messages him
instead, and the order page and status thread never happen. So it sends people back
to where their thing lives — *waiting on a piece?* → your order page; *want a scene
of your own?* → Only yours — and only then catches what is genuinely left over.

**Order access — settled in Round 5.** Order pages are reachable only by an
unguessable link and there are no accounts at launch.

- **A 128-bit token in the path** — `/o/<token>` — never a query string, never a
  sequential id. Stored raw so the same link can be re-sent; **rotatable in one
  dashboard click**; **never expiring**, because the alteration window runs from
  Delivered and past orders must always resolve.
- **The link goes out again with every status change**, so a customer always has a
  recent copy. Recovery stays human at launch: name and piece, and he sends it back.
- **Email and phone are both required.** Email is the automated rail; phone is
  delivery, alteration, and his own hand.
- **Never logged** — scrubbed from error reporting, and the analytics beacon is not
  rendered on order routes, because the path *is* the credential. `noindex, nofollow`
  and `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer` on those routes.
- **A customer row exists from day one**, keyed on phone, with auth columns null —
  so he can see *this is her third piece* on the day the order screen wants it,
  without anyone having an account. *(The dashboard's Customers section was cut in the
  pre-build review; the row was not.)*
- **A child is never an entity** *(R13a).* No child record, no name, no date of birth.
  A piece cut for a child is the parent's order, measured by a `guardian`, with age
  recorded as a number rather than a birth date — a birth date ages by itself and
  identifies. Measurements attached to no identified child is the difference between
  minimal data and a profile, and it is true from the first migration because it is
  not retrofittable once the shape exists.
- **Accounts arrive in Phase 8**, claimed after an order rather than before it, by
  magic link. Phone identifies; email authenticates. The token never dies — an
  account is another door to the same page, never the only one.

**WhatsApp is never automated** *(Round 5, amended in Round 11).* It appears on the
contact page and on order pages, **never site-wide** — a footer button is a permanent
invitation to leave the site, and within a month the order journey becomes a
formality wrapped around a chat. In Rwanda it is how commerce happens, so being
precious about it costs sales.

*Automating it was examined and rejected on cost, not taste:* WhatsApp's platform
bills per business-initiated message outside an open 24-hour service window, which
is exactly what a status update is. **His own thumb is free.** So the rule is about
the robot, not the channel: a hand-sent `wa.me` link needs no Business API, no
billing and no approval, and it *does* carry the token and the status. Every queue
row that sends an email offers a one-tap WhatsApp version beside it — same wording,
same link, the customer's locale, written by us and sent by him.

**The customer chooses.** A preferred-channel field on the order, email or WhatsApp.
**The email fires either way** — it is the permanent address of the token, and a chat
thread is not durable storage; chats get cleared and phones get replaced. Choosing
WhatsApp turns that queue row from optional into required. Said on the form rather
than discovered: *we'll message you on WhatsApp, and email you a copy you can always
come back to.*

*Round 5's original wording — "enquiry only, never a link, a status or a payment" —
was over-broad. Email is the automated rail because it is free, not because it is
what a Kigali customer checks.*

**Footer** — sits under the running-stitch divider, so the page ends on the site's
own heartbeat. Carries the philosophy line, so it underwrites every page without
being repeated in body copy. The handwritten script gets its one non-logo
appearance here: the site opens with the signature writing itself and closes with
it at rest.

**Open decision — the email list.** Nothing currently captures anyone not ready to
order, which at launch is nearly everyone. Collections release over time; without a
list, every release starts from zero and depends on the algorithm. In Ubuntu's
language it is an invitation into Abantu — *"Be told when the next collection
arrives"* — one field, no incentive, no popup, ever. **Recommended, not yet ruled on.**

## 12. The admin dashboard *(settled in Round 9)*

**Four sections: Today · Orders · Pieces · Settings.** Garment types — their
measurement lists and guardrail bands — live inside Settings, because he sets up three
and does not touch them for months, and the **makers list** sits beside them because
Round 16's foreign key has to surface somewhere.

**Customers was cut in the pre-build review.** It was the only one of the five with no
specification in any document, and an unspecified section is one that gets invented at
build time. R5's customer row is untouched. *"This is her third piece"* is one query
on the order screen's customer panel, on the day it is wanted.

**Today is a work queue, and it is what he lands on.** An order appears when the
next move is his, and leaves the moment he makes it. Gate-based, never time-based:
there is no threshold to guess, so it cannot nag and it cannot go quiet. It is
**never truncated** — everything waiting on him, grouped by action with a count per
heading, so twelve items read as four decisions rather than a wall. Days-waiting
sits on every row as information and changes nothing. One line at the foot counts
the orders waiting on *customers*, which is what feeds Lapsed.

The split inside it matters: **facts complete in place** — payment landed, package
posted, one tap and the email fires — while **judgements open the order.** Confirming
a price or sharing a design sends a stranger a number that cannot be withdrawn, and
a one-tap approve on that is exactly how a wrong price gets sent. No bulk action
anywhere on the site.

**The order screen is panels, not a timeline** — customer, money, fit, photos,
maker, with the event log collapsed into one of them. The queue arrives with a
specific job; making him read three weeks of history to find the button would undo
it. Two things it enforces: **price is adjustable at confirmation with a required
reason**, written as its own line in the snapshot so *why 51,000?* stays answerable;
and **a fit check is required before *In the making***, unconditionally, because
cloth is cut at the second gate and a wrong measurement found afterwards cannot be
undone.

**Pieces are drafts until he publishes them.** Phase 2 hands him the dashboard
before the public site exists, so he will spend weeks with half-written stories and
missing photos — draft means none of it leaks, and publish is a deliberate act with
a floor: photo, price, scene line, alt text. Commission-kind pieces sit in the same
section behind a filter, reachable for the clone action and past-commission stories
without burying the four window-display choices.

**Email bodies are code, not fields.** A body edited here would have no key and no
translation, silently becoming English-only where the flip bar cannot see it. He
gets a **personal note** instead — optional, above the signature, in whatever
language he is writing — on Confirmed, Declined, design shared and On its way.

- **Hand-coded and fixed (ours):** design, motion, page structure, every fixed
  string, the fit diagrams, measurement definitions, and all email bodies.
- **His to edit:** pieces (photos with focal point and alt text, names, translations,
  prices and modifiers, scene lines, ~150-word stories, colourways, cuts, making
  days), the window display, the collection theme, the order pipeline (confirm price,
  record payments at each gate, add progress photos), guardrail ranges, the queue and
  priority offsets, the commission design fee and reply time, past-commission
  stories, Abantu entries, the **makers list** in Settings, and the maker chosen on
  each order.
- ~~**Read-only:** translation completeness per piece.~~ **Cut in the pre-build
  review** — a meter of progress toward a switch that is off. The flip bar is three
  named things and is checked by hand. The email bodies stay keyed in both locales;
  the keys are cheap and the missing Kinyarwanda values are the unbuilt part.
- **Discipline:** the dashboard stays minimal — his content and his orders, **not a
  full website builder.** Test: if he would want to change it monthly, it is a
  field; if changing it would change the design, it is code.

**Removal, not deletion.** A piece with orders can be **removed from the site** —
gone from every public page, unorderable — while every past order still resolves to
it. **Hard delete only for a piece with zero orders** (wrong photo, duplicate,
typo). One button, two behaviours, decided by the data rather than by him
remembering a rule. Removal is reversible in one click.

**How he gets in** *(Round 12).* Password **and** magic link, both, every login —
about monthly, because the session runs thirty days. The password is the one factor
his phone does not contain, which is the whole reason it exists, and it is never
saved in the browser. *Sign out everywhere* works from any other device and takes
effect instantly. The dashboard pins to his home screen like an app, but it is a
website and the phone's own lock screen still sits in front of it.

**Two free things outside the code carry more weight than anything in it:** his email
recovery must exist somewhere other than that phone, and his photos must back up
automatically — nothing here keeps an original, so his phone is the archive.
